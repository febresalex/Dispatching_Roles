Speak the language 📖

Every team has its own vocabulary. Here's yours. You'll hear these terms thrown around constantly, so let's get them locked in.

Delivery Scheduler
The system tool you'll basically live in. It's where you view, assign, route, and lock every delivery to a truck and date.
Soft Schedule
Delivery is set for a date, but no specific time. Flexible.
Think: "Sometime Tuesday."
Hard Schedule
Delivery is locked to a specific date AND time. Non-negotiable.
Think: "Tuesday at 10 AM sharp."
Map Zone
A geographic grouping that helps cluster stops together so trucks aren't zigzagging across town.
Unscheduled Orders
Orders that are ready to be routed but haven't been assigned yet. Your daily to-do list.
Locked Order
An order that's been finalized in the scheduler. Don't touch it unless you have authorization.
PAI
The system that sends automated delivery confirmations and reminders to customers.
Split Invoice
When items on one order are delivered or installed separately. Requires its own little scheduling dance.
Delivery Window
The time range you promise the customer. Realistic + non-overlapping = happy customers.
Route Optimization
The art of sequencing stops so trucks aren't wasting time. Less driving = more deliveries = more revenue.

Your mission, should you choose to accept it

Dispatch Team

You're the air traffic controller for every appliance that leaves our warehouse. Here's what's on your plate:

  • Managing and maintaining the Delivery Scheduler (it's your domain)
  • Assigning orders to trucks and clearing the Unscheduled Orders list daily
  • Planning and optimizing delivery routes by map zone
  • Reviewing customer notes for time specifications, requested installers, special instructions, and equipment needs
  • Sending confirmations and reminders via PAI
  • Coordinating reschedules and customer follow-ups
  • Supporting clean, accurate invoicing
🚨
The #1 dispatch sin
Skipping customer notes. A note might say "client requested James as installer" or "deliver before 11 AM — concierge access only." Miss it, and you're sending a truck on a wasted trip. Always. Read. The. Notes.
4

Scheduling deliveries like a pro

Here's the bulk-scheduling workflow you'll run when you're routing deliveries in the Delivery Scheduler. Follow it in order — each step builds on the last.

  1. Pull up all orders ready to route in the Delivery Scheduler.
  2. Pick your delivery date — the one you're scheduling for.
  3. Sort orders by map zone so you can group nearby stops together.
  4. Assign unassigned orders to the right trucks (zone, capacity, installer needs).
  5. Sweep the Unscheduled Orders list and schedule anything still hanging out.
  6. Verify with the color check — confirm orders are assigned by looking at the truck color display.
  7. Assign delivery times with realistic, non-overlapping windows.
  8. Lock everything once scheduling is final.
  9. Double-check each truck for time conflicts, route flow, and capacity.
🔍
Need to find a delivery later?
You can search by customer name, order number, delivery date, or assigned truck. Four ways in — no excuse to lose track of an order.
Pro tip

Build routes the way a luxury concierge plans an evening — every stop should flow logically into the next. If you find yourself zigzagging across map zones, stop and re-zone. Your drivers (and your fuel budget) will thank you.

5

Soft vs. Hard schedules: know the difference

When scheduling directly on an order (instead of bulk-scheduling), the big decision is: Soft or Hard? Get this right, and the rest falls into place.

  1. Confirm product availability before promising any date. Never offer what you can't deliver.
  2. Call the customer to confirm their preferred date.
  3. Open the order and select the Delivery Date field.
  4. Choose Soft or Hard (see below 👇).
  5. Assign a specific truck and time if you have permission to.
  6. Save the order. Done.
🌤
Soft Schedule

Date is set, time is flexible. Use when the client is easy-going about timing.

"Tuesday works great, anytime!"
⏰
Hard Schedule

Date AND time are locked in. Use when the client has a specific window. Always takes priority during routing.

"Has to be Tuesday between 10 and 11 AM."
⚠️
Don't over-promise on Hard schedules
Every Hard schedule constrains the rest of your route. Three Hards in one zone? Fine. Three Hards across three different zones? You're setting up your drivers (and your customers) for a bad day.

7

When plans change: reschedules & updates

Life happens. Trucks break down. Customers go on vacation. Contractors push installs. Your job isn't to prevent reschedules — it's to handle them so smoothly the customer barely notices the change.

  1. Take the request (via phone or email).
  2. Confirm the new date and time with the customer before doing anything else.
  3. Update the order with the new delivery info.
  4. Re-assign to the right truck for the new date.
  5. Lock the updated schedule.
  6. Send a new confirmation via PAI so the customer has it in writing.
  7. Document the reason in the order notes — future-you will thank present-you.
⚠️
Split invoices need extra love
When you're splitting an invoice, follow up with the customer to schedule the affected items, assign them to the right truck, set delivery times, lock the order, AND send a new confirmation — all before finalizing the invoice. Don't skip steps; you'll create more work later.
8

Returns & damaged items: handle with care

Sometimes an appliance arrives damaged. Sometimes the client changes their mind. Either way, how we respond determines whether they buy from us again.

  • Document the return or damage in the system immediately
  • Loop in the right departments (operations, warehouse, sales)
  • Arrange pickups or replacements as needed
  • Make sure invoice adjustments reflect what actually happened
🚨
Document first, act second
An undocumented return is an invoicing time bomb. The customer might be charged for something they returned, or we might lose the item in the system entirely. Always record before you do anything else.
9

Finishing strong: finalizing invoices

An invoice is the last impression you leave on a transaction. Make it clean. Walk through this checklist before you finalize anything:

  • Delivery completion confirmed ✓
  • All delivery details accurate in the system ✓
  • Returns and damages documented and reflected ✓
  • Split invoice items addressed and scheduled ✓
  • Invoice matches Fuse billing procedures ✓
🏆

Win of the day

A clean, accurate invoice on the first pass is one of the most satisfying things in dispatch. No callbacks. No corrections. Just done. Aim for this every single time.

10

Daily QC ritual ☕

Before you wrap up each day, run through this quick check. Five minutes here saves hours of cleanup tomorrow.

  • All today's orders are scheduled and assigned
  • Trucks are balanced (no one's overloaded while another sits empty)
  • Routes are optimized by map zone
  • Customers have been notified (confirmations + reminders sent)
  • Zero time conflicts on any truck
  • Unscheduled Orders list reviewed and cleared
☕
Make it a habit

Some of our best dispatchers run their QC review with their morning coffee or end-of-day wrap-up. Find your rhythm — just don't skip it.
